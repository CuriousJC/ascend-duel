# Icon button art prompt

A set of **square icon buttons**: each one a whole button — the rounded face and the picture on it —
drawn together as one image. They carry no text; the picture is the button's whole meaning.

Six jade buttons, and three of them drawn a second time in **amber**, so nine files in all.

---

## The prompt

High-resolution digital illustration of **square game-interface buttons**, one per file, for a
dark-fantasy card game. Each is a rounded square of **polished stone** with one pale icon on it.

### The button

- **A square with rounded corners**, the corner radius about **a fifth of the side**. Clearly
  rounded, not a circle and not a pill.
- **Polished stone**: smooth and dense, with **faint soft clouding** inside it — paler and darker
  drifts of the stone's own color, like real carved jade. The clouding is gentle and low-contrast; it
  must never compete with the icon. Not glass, not plastic, not metal.
- **The face is one smooth gradient and nothing else**: the stone's `light` value at the top, its
  `core` across the middle, its `dark` at the bottom. A soft satin polish, as if lit from the upper
  left.
- **The face is completely plain apart from the icon.** **Do not draw any line, arc, band, rim,
  ridge, groove, highlight stripe, inner border or second outline anywhere on the face** — not along
  the top, not along the bottom, not around the icon. No hard gloss, no glass reflection, no sparkle.
  The only thing on the stone besides its gentle clouding is the icon.
- **No heavy outline.** The edge of the button is a **thin, soft dark line — about a fortieth of the
  side**, in the stone's own `dark`, not black. No drop shadow, no black ring, no inked comic-book
  contour.
- **The button fills the canvas**: its edge touches all four sides, with no padding and no shadow
  falling outside it. Everything outside the rounded corners is **fully transparent**.

### The icon

- **Centered, filling about 70% of the button's width**, so there is a clear margin of stone all
  round it.
- **Pale ivory, against the stone**: the icon is light and the button is mid-dark, so it reads by
  contrast at a glance. Smooth shading within the icon — lighter toward the upper left, a little
  darker away from it — and a **thin darker contour** of its own, about a fiftieth of the side:
  enough to hold its edge, nowhere near heavy.
- **Every icon is the same ivory, on both stones.** No icon takes a color of its own — the flame is
  not orange, the sword is not steel, the cog is not gray. They are one set, told apart by silhouette
  alone.
- **It sits on the face, not above it**: no cast shadow onto the button, no glow, no halo.
- **Chunky shapes only.** Nothing may be thinner than about **a twelfth of the side**: the buttons
  are used at **44 to 68 pixels** across, so a thin line becomes a scratch and a small detail
  disappears. Each icon is recognized by its silhouette first.

### The two stones

Every icon is drawn on **jade**. The three sort icons are drawn a second time on **amber** — the
same button in every way but the stone: same size, same corner radius, same gradient, same icon in
exactly the same place at exactly the same size. Only the color of the stone changes, so swapping
one file for the other never makes the button move.

The three sort buttons sit side by side as one group, and exactly one of them is amber at any moment:
the amber one is the choice in force. So the amber has to stand out from the two jade beside it at a
glance, and its icon must still be the first thing the eye finds on it.

- **Jade** — a deep, slightly blue-leaning green. Not emerald, not grass green, not lime.
- **Amber** — warm honey-gold polished amber, like the fossil resin: rich and deep, not yellow, not
  orange, not brown, not bright. Its clouding is the same soft drifting of paler and darker amber.

**All nine files are the same button.** Draw the jade tile once and put each icon on a copy of it;
draw the amber tile once and put each sort icon on a copy of that. The clouding should be the same
on every copy of each stone, so the buttons read as one set.

### The canvas

Each file is **exactly 256 x 256**, a **transparent** PNG with an alpha channel. Work as large as you
like natively and reduce once to 256 on the way out.

Avoid: text, letters, numbers, card suits, labels, any line or arc or band on the button's face, a
frame or inner border, a rim highlight, a second button, a background, scenery, sparkle, glow, hard
gloss, bevel edges, colored icons, bright or saturated green, yellow or orange, and any heavy black
outline.
