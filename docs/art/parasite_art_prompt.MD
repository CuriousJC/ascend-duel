# Parasite shapes

**The one thing a parasite prompt needs that a ring's does not.** The prompt itself is
[card_art_prompt.MD](card_art_prompt.MD) — pasted with its **upper band** composition block,
because a parasite draws through `cards.WormStyle` and the text band covers everything below
y 140. This file is the body plan, pasted between the two.

**Why it exists.** A ring is an object and can be described from nothing. A parasite is a
*creature*, and a generator handed "a parasite" with no body plan invents a different animal
every time — which is how a catalogue of thirty-seven ends up looking like thirty-seven
separate commissions.

## Shape — one entry, pasted verbatim

**Closed list. Pick exactly one.** A parasite that does not fit one of these is a signal to
argue for an eleventh entry, not a licence to write a new body plan inline — an inline shape is
the whole failure this file exists to prevent.

- **Boring beetle** — a hard-shelled beetle with a long drilling snout and ridged wing cases,
  legs tucked close to a compact body.
- **Grub** — a soft, legless, deeply segmented larva with a blunt darker head capsule, curled
  into a shallow C.
- **Scarab** — a domed, armoured beetle with heavy ridged elytra and short splayed digging legs.
- **Tick** — a squat creature that is mostly swollen abdomen, with a tiny head and eight short
  legs clustered at the front.
- **Centipede** — a long, flat, many-segmented creature with a leg to each segment and a pair of
  fangs at the head.
- **Spider** — a compact cephalothorax and bulbous abdomen carried on eight angular legs, low to
  the ground.
- **Mantis** — an upright narrow thorax with folded raptorial forelimbs and a triangular head.
- **Wasp** — a sharply waisted body with folded wings held along the back and a visible sting.
- **Moth** — a furred body under broad scaled wings held flat and open to either side.
- **Casing** — not a living creature at all: a spun or chitinous cocoon, seam-ridged, sealed,
  with no visible limbs or head.

Paste the chosen entry, then add: the creature is presented broadside — head to one side, tail
to the other — and is drawn alone, with no host: never attached to a card, a limb or a body.

**Which family takes which is a design decision, not a guess.** The catalogue already names most
of them: the five `-bore` parasites are boring beetles, the four `-grub` are grubs, `Rock Beetle`
/ `Carapace` / `Shell` are scarabs, `Leech` / `Hunger` are ticks, and `Cocoon` / `Husk` / `Brood`
are casings. Everything else is open and is settled in the subject paragraph.

## The subject — written per parasite

Say what makes this one that creature and not its sibling — colour, ornament, what the effect is
doing to it. One paragraph. The prompt never names the parasite and the shape never
distinguishes two of a kind, so everything that tells `Emberbore` from `Rimebore` lives here.
