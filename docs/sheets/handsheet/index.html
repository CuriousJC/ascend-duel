Hand sheet

19 hands, cheapest-paying first. Deck of 55, 45 of them attacks; 5 cards per concept, 15 per form, 11 per element. A round has 6 action points and plays at most 5 cards. Mini card 102×140, shown at 1:1 on the table the hand is actually laid out on.

Regenerate with go run ./tools/handsheet and refresh. Every rung comes from internal/combat's live catalogue and every example is built out of cards that are really in data/duelist_cards.json — so a rung with no cards beside it is a rung the shipping deck cannot form at all.

Ordered by multiplier, across all three axes at once. That is the comparison the numbers are making — an Elemental Three of a Kind at 195 is claiming to be worth about what a Form Full House is not — and it is the comparison hands.json's axis-by-axis layout hides.

The AP figure is not the difficulty, and the reachability is. The AP is what the example costs once you hold the cards. Reachable is how often you hold them: of 2000000 opening hands of 8 dealt off this deck, the share that could afford some set forming the rung — which is the figure the multipliers are priced against. Best in hand is the share where it was the dearest-paying rung available, which is what a player taking the most they could would actually have landed.

Round one only: a later hand draws from a depleted pile and keeps what it did not spend, which the sample does not model. 0.000% of hands build no rung at all — the turns the High Card names. The same table, with the axes kept apart and an -ap flag for a turn holding cost discounts, is go run ./tools/handodds: one pinned sample in tools/hands, printed by both, so the two cannot disagree.

6 of these examples cost more than a round has, on action points or on the card cap. That is the example, not the rung: each one varies everything the rung does not count, so it is picked to illustrate rather than to be cheap. Every rung on this ladder has some build a 6 AP round can pay for — an Elemental Five of a Kind is Jab, Cut, Bash, Ward and Thrust in one colour, 6 AP exactly.
