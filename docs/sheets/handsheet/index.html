Hand sheet

25 hands, cheapest-paying first. Deck of 55, 45 of them attacks; 5 cards per concept, 15 per form, 11 per element. A round has 6 action points and plays at most 5 cards. Mini card 102×140, shown at 1:1 on the table the hand is actually laid out on.

Regenerate with go run ./tools/handsheet and refresh. Every rung comes from internal/combat's live catalogue and every example is built out of cards that are really in data/duelist_cards.json — so a rung with no cards beside it is a rung the shipping deck cannot form at all.

Ordered by multiplier, across every axis at once. That is the comparison the numbers are making — an Elemental Three of a Kind is claiming to be worth about what a Weaponmaster is — and it is the comparison hands.json's axis-by-axis layout hides.

The AP figure is not the difficulty, and the three percentages are. The AP is what the example costs once you hold the cards. Of 2000000 opening hands of 8 dealt off this deck, dealt is the share holding the cards for the rung at all, and playable is the share that could also pay for them inside the round's action points. They come apart hard on the five-card rungs: a Form Full House is dealt in most hands and payable in very few.

The score is the geometric mean of the two, and it is what the multipliers are priced against. Pricing on the deal alone would put a five-card Full House below a two-card pair, since the shuffle hands it to you constantly and the round cannot pay for it; pricing on playability alone would treat a rung you are dealt every hand and can rarely afford as though you had never seen it, when in fact it is a hand you can plan toward. The curve behind it is tools/hands, and go run ./tools/handodds -price prints what it would charge for each rung beside what the file charges today. Best in hand is the share where the rung was the dearest-paying one available — what a player taking the most they could would actually have landed.

Round one only: a later hand draws from a depleted pile and keeps what it did not spend, which the sample does not model. 0.000% of hands build no rung at all — the turns the High Card names. The same table, with the axes kept apart and an -ap flag for a turn holding cost discounts, is go run ./tools/handodds: one pinned sample in tools/hands, printed by both, so the two cannot disagree.

12 of these examples cost more than a round has, on action points or on the card cap. That is the example, not the rung: each one varies everything the rung does not count, so it is picked to illustrate rather than to be cheap. Every rung on this ladder has some build a 6 AP round can pay for — an Elemental Five of a Kind is Jab, Cut, Bash, Ward and Thrust in one colour, 6 AP exactly.
