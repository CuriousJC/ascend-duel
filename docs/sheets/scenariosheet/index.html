Scenario sheet

The 44 fixtures in internal/scenario/scenarios.json: what each one plugs in, and the command that launches it. Compiled out of every build that is not -tags scenario, so none of this is reachable from a shipped binary.

The cards are drawn by internal/cards, the same code the game blits — but plain. A rider is printed as the word the file writes rather than painted onto the face; what an upgrade looks like is the upgrade sheet's subject.

a-full-rowfirst in the file — the bare launch
opens on combat · room 0 · against GiantBat · DUMMY -- nothing dies · 12 AP a turn · 50 relic slots
ASCEND_DUEL_SCENARIO=a-full-row go run -tags scenario .

Fifty of everything: fifty relics worn, fifty runes in the sack, fifty cards in the hand. It is the stress test for the three rows that share one widget - the hand, the worn relics and the sack are the same draggable, overlapping row at three widths - and it is kept rather than deleted because how they behave when full is a question worth looking at again. Each row packs inside its own pane, so none of them can squeeze another; resting the cursor on any of them raises the card under it whole, on the same tick its tooltip appears; and any of the three can be dragged into a different order. Fifty relics is possible at all because there is no ceiling on how many a duelist may wear - combat.MaxWornRelics was deleted on 2026-09-17 and what a run wears is combat.DefaultRelicSlots or whatever the run is carrying, which this sets to fifty. Nothing here is tuned: fifty relics firing into one sum is a picture of a layout rather than a balance question.

The Card Full House, dealt on purpose: three Cuts and two Braces out of a hand of eight, which is 5 AP and pays 3.42x. It is the hand that says most about how the ladder is counted, because both halves of it are things a player has to be told. The rung matches on CONCEPT - data/hands.json writes match concept, groups [3, 2] - so the three Cuts are a set because they are Cuts, and the colors they happen to wear are worth nothing to it; recolor every card in this hand and the multiplier does not move. And the two Braces are DEFEND cards that join the hand like anything else: they carry a form and an element, they count toward the rung, and they bring no damage with them - so the sum is three 0.5x slashes under a 3.42x multiplier, and the shields go up beside the blow rather than instead of it. The other three cards - a Flinch, a Jab and a Block - are there to be left behind, so the hand is a choice rather than the only five cards in the row. Giant Bat, 15 AP and a dummy, and it shares the shields fixture's deck so the refill behaves the same way. See ASCEND_DUEL_SCENARIO=shields for the defences on their own.

opens on combat · room 0 · against GiantBat · DUMMY -- nothing dies · 15 AP a turn

The shields, on their own: what a defend card raises, what one eats, and how the break is drawn. No relics at all, so nothing on the screen is multiplying or recoloring anything and the only thing moving is the defence. The deck is two thirds defend cards across all five colors - Flinch at 0 AP, Brace at 1, Block at 2, Guard at 3 - with a thin seam of attacks so a turn can still form a hand and the shields can be watched going up beside a blow rather than instead of one. 15 AP, so a turn is a choice of which five cards rather than which five are affordable; remember combat.MaxActions still caps the turn at five cards however cheap they are, and one card raises at most five shields while a duelist holds as many as the turn paid for. The opponent is a Giant Bat because its deck has three clearly different blows - Nip at 50, Swoop at 100 and Drain at 200 - which is what makes the rule visible: a shield eats the creature's HEAVIEST blow, not its first, and it decides that at the top of the creature's turn. Watch the beat between the two turns, where the blocked pips fly out of the duelist card into the card they kill and the crack opens across its face. The pip row along the bottom of the duelist card draws at most six - screens.maxShieldPips - so a turn that raises ten draws a full row and the true count is on the engine. Against a dummy, so it can be watched as many times as it takes rather than survived.

flip-cascade
opens on combat · room 0 · DUMMY -- nothing dies · 15 AP a turn
ASCEND_DUEL_SCENARIO=flip-cascade go run -tags scenario .

The deal, the flip cascade and the sort, which is how every hand in a fight now arrives - see screens/combat_deal.go. Frozen Lightning, Glacier and Permafrost are worn in that order, so the cascade CHAINS and then doubles back: lightning goes to ice under ring one, ice goes to earth under ring two, and earth comes back to ice under ring three. Every lightning, ice and earth card in the deck therefore lands on ice, and a card can be watched going ice, earth, ice across three beats - which is the thing three rings are here to show, because a run wearing the middle relic alone would end on earth. The fire cards are the bystanders: no ring names them, so they sit still through all three beats and the row has something to be compared against. What to watch is the order - cards fly out of the pile left to right in the order the shuffle gave them, then each ring in turn rattles, rocks and lights while every card it touches rattles and morphs at once, and only then does the row sort itself. Press E for the element sort, which is where sorting LAST earns itself: under the default cost sort the element is only a tiebreak and the row barely moves, where on E it reorganises around what the cascade just did. Play a turn and the refill runs the whole sequence again over the cards it deals. Against a dummy, 15 AP, so it can be watched as many times as it takes rather than survived.

life-relics
opens on combat · room 0 · against Basilisk · 10 AP a turn · 20-round clock
ASCEND_DUEL_SCENARIO=life-relics go run -tags scenario .

Two flips that chain and the two life relics, on a real duelist with a readable health bar. The deck is half lightning and half ice; Frozen Lightning is worn left of Meltdown, so a lightning card is dealt ice and then fire - two steps, two beats, a toast on each ring - while a native ice card takes the second step alone. That is combat.FlipSteps carrying a running element through the worn list, and the worn order is what decides it: swap the two rings and lightning stays lightning, because Meltdown reads the card before Frozen Lightning has touched it. Vampiric Fang gives back 3/10 of every blow and The Tear restores 1/4 of maximum life at the top of each turn, so the two life gestures are side by side: a drain leaves the body it was taken out of, a regeneration leaves the ring that made it. It has NO Hand on purpose - a plugged hand is seated straight out of the run and never goes through drawHand, so it would show the cards unflipped and the cascade would not run at all. It is not a dummy for the same kind of reason: 999999 life each way makes both life figures land on a bar that cannot visibly move. The Rabbit's Foot is worn last, so every gold and silver card in the deck reads 2 IN 5 rather than 1 IN 5 on its tooltip and pays that often - and if anything shocks the duelist, their own miss chance reads 1/2 rather than 1/4, which is the drawback the relic is honest about. Round one heals nothing either way, since nothing in the game heals above full.
