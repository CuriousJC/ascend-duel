Upgrade sheet

All 10 visible upgrades a card can carry, each drawn on all four form marks. The card is cards.Hand, 203×280; the form mark's box is 32px at (10,10); the ticks are 20×5. Every ink is 32×32; a washed card goes 55% of the way toward it and a washed border 80%. The game draws wash-face.

A card has a form, an element and an action — and then one upgrade. A second upgrade replaces the first outright. Spec.Element is still what the card is, and the left column still states it; the wildcard is the one upgrade that takes the hue off the form mark, because it is the one whose subject is that the card counts as every element at once.

Three styles, and the question is which one to keep. border paints the 3px ring and leaves the face alone — the border was freed up in August when the element moved off it, and what is left in that slot is the card's state, which is a wash away from neutral rather than a hue. wash takes the whole card, border included: nobody misses a gold card, and nothing on the face is unaffected. wash-face is the middle answer. Read them against the plain row above, and against a whole hand rather than one card — five loud cards in a row is the failure mode the border style exists to avoid.

Eight of these colours are placeholders. Hue is spent — five elements, the ring pink, the two verbs, the two duelists, the ground — so what is here is picked to be told apart rather than to mean anything. Gold and silver are the exception: they are metals, and they are what the mechanic is called. Retune the rest in systems.upgradeTint and re-run.

Each upgrade below carries the tooltip a card wearing it actually shows, built by internal/carddesc — the same call screens.cardTip makes, not a copy of it. The card is a fire Lunge and the duelist behind it hits for 10, so a figure that looks wrong can be checked by eye. There are no rings on, which is why the block is the whole panel: the game appends its damage chain only when a ring has moved something. The colouring is the game's too — the same cards.ElementRuns table the screen reads — which is why FIRE is red and CHROMATIC is not: the wheel has no hue left for "all of them".
